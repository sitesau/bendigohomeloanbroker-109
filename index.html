Mortgage Broker Bendigo Checklist Explained

Before comparing lenders, write one sentence that defines the job: buying a first home near Kangaroo Flat or Strathdale, refinancing in Golden Square, structuring an Epsom or Eaglehawk investment, or securing pre-approval for a search. That sentence controls the documents requested, the features worth pricing and the risks worth testing. Borrowers weighing Mortgage Broker Bendigo Suburb Guide for home buyers can use the same purpose statement to keep suburb choice, budget and lender fit aligned.

  • First purchase: test deposit position, assistance pathways, LMI exposure and how pre-approval should sit behind an offer.
  • Refinance: ask whether rate, consolidation or renovation equity improves the position after switching costs and features.
  • Investment: compare principal and interest with interest only, then check how offset use and existing equity are treated by policy.
  • Pre-approval: confirm validity, conditions that can alter it, and the checks still required once a property is found.

Build the file before the first lender conversation

The strongest applications begin with evidence assembled in one folder. A broker can only test lender appetite when income, identity, conduct, liabilities and target property are visible. Ask for a document list early so gaps are fixed before a contract, valuation or cooling-off period creates pressure.

  1. Income: recent payslips, employment details and any variable or self-employed evidence the lender may request.
  2. Identity and residency: the ID forms accepted by the lender, kept consistent across applications.
  3. Savings conduct: statements showing genuine saving and account behaviour.
  4. Existing debts: current loan statements, limits, repayments and closed accounts where relevant.
  5. Property goal: target suburb, price range, purchase purpose and whether renovation or investment use is planned.

Compare structure, not only the headline number

A cheaper rate can still be the wrong loan when structure is ignored. Use a comparison grid that records repayment type, rate type, offset access, redraw, fees, equity use, LMI and policy constraints. Ask the broker to explain the trade-off behind each recommendation instead of presenting a ranked list.

  • Fixed versus variable: weigh certainty against flexibility and break costs if plans change.
  • Offset account: ask whether linked funds reduce interest in a way that suits cash flow.
  • Interest only versus principal and interest: match the structure to owner-occupier or investor purpose and repayment capacity.
  • Equity and consolidation: test whether accessing value or combining debts changes risk, term and total cost.
  • LMI: check when it applies, how loan-to-value ratio affects it and whether a smaller or different purchase changes the outcome.

Governance questions that protect the borrower

Licensing and disclosure belong in the first meeting, not after a recommendation. Confirm that the broker holds an Australian Credit Licence or acts as a credit representative under a licensee, and ask whether the firm belongs to AFCA for external dispute resolution. Request an explanation of remuneration: most Australian residential brokers are paid lender commission rather than a direct borrower fee, while exceptions must be disclosed upfront.

Ask how the best interests duty is applied in practice. Since 1 January 2021, brokers providing credit assistance must act in the consumer's best interests and prioritise the consumer. That should appear as a reasoned recommendation with alternatives, limits and plain-language trade-offs.

  • Which lenders were considered and why were others excluded?
  • What borrower fees, commissions or volume benefits apply?
  • What could make this recommendation unsuitable later?
  • How are complaints handled if the borrower disagrees?

Sequence the timeline from pre-approval to keys

Treat timing as a workflow. Pre-approval is commonly useful for about 90 days but remains conditional. Formal approval still depends on the complete application, verification and the selected property. From application to settlement, allow roughly four to six weeks where the lender and circumstances cooperate, while treating that range as planning guidance rather than a promise.

  1. Define the goal and gather evidence before any lender submission.
  2. Obtain conditional approval and note expiry, assumptions and required updates.
  3. After a property is found, provide contract, valuation and property details promptly.
  4. Track outstanding conditions until formal approval, then coordinate settlement steps.

Delays are often administrative rather than financial: missing statements, unclear employment evidence, undisclosed debts or property information that arrives late.

  1. Define the loan purpose. State whether the aim is a first purchase, refinance, investment loan or pre-approval before lender options are compared.
  2. Assemble evidence. Collect income, identity, savings, existing loan and property information so lender fit can be tested early.
  3. Test structure and cost. Ask how repayment type, rate type, offset access, equity use and LMI change the recommendation.
  4. Confirm governance. Check licensing, AFCA access, remuneration disclosure and how best interests duty shaped the advice.
  5. Manage conditions to settlement. Track pre-approval expiry, formal approval requirements and document requests through to settlement.

Common questions

What should a Bendigo borrower bring to a first broker meeting? Bring one clear goal plus income evidence, ID, savings statements, existing loan details and property information where available. The purpose is to let the broker test lender fit, likely conditions and timing gaps before a purchase contract creates urgency.

Is the lowest rate always the best home loan recommendation? No. The recommendation should weigh repayment type, fixed or variable rate, offset access, redraw, fees, equity use, LMI and lender policy. A lower headline rate may carry trade-offs that do not suit the borrower's stated goal.

How long should pre-approval and settlement be planned for? Pre-approval is typically useful for around 90 days, depending on the lender. Application to settlement is commonly about four to six weeks, depending on lender processing and the borrower's circumstances. Both figures are planning ranges, not guarantees.
